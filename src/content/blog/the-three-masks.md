My therapist would probably have thoughts about this, but hear me out.

I noticed I was context-switching poorly. I'd be deep in a Rust borrow checker fight, someone would message me about a job application, and suddenly I'm trying to be "professional" while my brain is still in systems mode. The gears grind.

So I made my AI do the switching for me.

## Three Contexts, One Mind

Now I have three contexts. Same underlying setup, different instructions:

**Work mode** is for job hunting and professional positioning. It knows about the 5K Obsidian users, the CTF progression, the $20K I saved Codewave. When I paste a job description, it constructs a narrative that makes 8 years of building sound like the asset it actually is.

**Dev mode** is where we break things. This is the context that knows about Dokusho, that suggests I try Tauri, that gets excited about CTF challenges. It's less about outcomes, more about the craft.

**Personal mode** is... quieter. Sometimes I just need to think out loud without an agenda. This context keeps the long view, remembers that I'm more than my GitHub graph.

## How It Works

The folders are stupid simple:

```
work/      # resumes, cover letters, "hire me" energy
dev/       # side projects, experiments, "what if?" energy
personal/  # the rest
```

Each has a README explaining the vibe. The AI reads it before responding.

## Does This Actually Help?

I spend less mental energy figuring out which version of myself to be, and more energy on the actual problem.

That feels like a win.
