I have 8 years of experience building things. Resumes say 2-3 years "professional." The gap is full of side projects, CTFs, hackathons, tools I built because I needed them.

For a long time, I just... didn't apply to places. Felt like lying to claim 8 years, felt like selling short to claim 3.

Then I started using AI differently.

![](https://res.cloudinary.com/dmqn4aaos/image/upload/v1739287200/blog/ai-resume-narrative.avif)

## The Real History

My AI setup knows my actual story. Not the sanitized version — the real mess. The 5,000 Obsidian users who found my Cloudinary plugin. The progression from 14th place at my first CTF to 4th at Hackfest. The architecture students who came back to thank me after my Stable Diffusion seminar.

When I paste a job description, it doesn't keyword-match. It constructs a narrative that makes sense of my actual trajectory.

- **Backend security role?** Here's why someone who thinks like an attacker to protect users fits.
- **AI/ML position?** Here's how teaching Stable Diffusion to non-technical architects shows communication skills.
- **Full-stack job?** Here's the 8-year through-line from Node.js experiments to production systems.

## What Actually Works

The magic isn't the AI writing for me. It's the AI helping me see which stories fit which contexts. I have more material than I thought. I just couldn't see how to tell it.

Specificity beats generic every time:
- "Led seminars" → "Taught Stable Diffusion to architecture faculty — they came back to thank me"
- "5K users" → "5K users because I felt the pain and built the tool I needed"

## The Limit

Does it work? Early days. But I'm getting interviews I wouldn't have applied for six months ago.

The technical rounds are still me — no AI in the world can whiteboard for you. But at least I'm making it to the room.

That feels like progress.
